I am a sociology student. That is the plainest way to describe what I do, and it is the reason this blog exists.

Sociology is the discipline that starts from a stubborn premise: whatever looks like a private decision is usually also a public arrangement. The choice you call personal — how you spend an evening, whether you speak up in a meeting, what you find funny, what you find embarrassing — was assembled by a group you never met and never agreed to.

Psychology asks what happens inside a person. Philosophy asks what would have to be true for any of it to make sense. Sociology asks who set the conditions. I read all three, and what I write here is where they meet.

## The three questions I keep coming back to

Every piece I write is an attempt at one of these:

1. **Why do people do what they do?** Not the excuse, not the mechanism — the mechanism.
2. **Which parts of that behavior are actually ours to change?** Some of it is a choice, some of it is a habit, some of it is a room we were put in.
3. **What would change my mind?** The question I ask before I publish, and the one I want you to ask of me.

## The rules I hold myself to

**A claim without a check is a rumour.** If I write that something works, I name where the evidence came from: a study with a sample size, an observation with a limit, a book with an argument. If I cannot source it, I do not print it.

**A mechanism beats a label.** "He is lazy" explains nothing. "The task has no deadline, so there is no moment at which not doing it counts as a decision" explains a lot. Naming the mechanism is what turns a complaint into something you can test.

**Confidence is not evidence.** Certainty in the tone of voice is cheap and often inherited from the author rather than the subject. A careful sentence beats a loud one.

**I will say what I do not know.** Every piece here has an edge, and I try to mark it.

**The reader is not stupid.** I do not write to be impressive. I write so that someone can disagree with me precisely.

## What this is not

It is not a self-help blog. I am not going to tell you that your life will change if you wake up at five. When I write about habits, it is because the mechanism behind them is interesting and often misdescribed, not because I have a routine to sell.

It is also not an academic blog. I am a student, not a researcher. I read papers and books and try to compress them into something usable, and I say so when a topic is one I am still learning.

## Why write at all

Because the gap between what is written about behavior and what is actually true about it is enormous, and most of it is closed by repetition rather than by evidence. Every text repeats the previous text, and the repetition gets mistaken for the evidence.

Somebody has to check. That is the whole job. It is not glamorous, and it is exactly what a sociologist does: take a thing everybody accepts, ask who benefits from it being accepted, and go look.

## What I ask of you

Read the argument, not the conclusion. If I have made a claim you doubt, tell me which part you doubt and why. If you think the mechanism I named is the wrong one, say what you would name instead.

I will update the piece when you are right, and I will leave a note saying what changed.

Start anywhere. The piece under "Start here" is the closest thing I have to an introduction. Everything else is the same habit applied to a different question.
