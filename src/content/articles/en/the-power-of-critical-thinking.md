In an age of information overload, critical thinking is not just a skill—it's a survival mechanism.

## What Is Critical Thinking?

Critical thinking is the disciplined art of ensuring that you use the best thinking you are capable of in any set of circumstances. It involves:

- **Clarity**: Understanding exactly what is being said
- **Accuracy**: Checking if something is true
- **Precision**: Getting down to the specifics
- **Relevance**: Focusing on what matters
- **Depth**: Acknowledging complexity
- **Breadth**: Considering multiple perspectives
- **Logic**: Making sure things make sense together
- **Fairness**: Being open-minded and humble

## Why Most People Don't Think Critically

Our brains are wired for efficiency, not accuracy. We rely on mental shortcuts called **heuristics** that served our ancestors well but often lead us astray in the modern world.

### Common Cognitive Biases

| Bias | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Confirmation Bias | Seeking evidence that confirms our beliefs |
| Dunning-Kruger Effect | Overestimating our competence |
| Anchoring | Relying too heavily on the first piece of information |
| Availability Heuristic | Overestimating the importance of recent information |

## A Practical Framework

Here's a simple but powerful framework you can use for any decision:

1. **Identify the question** — What exactly are you trying to figure out?
2. **Gather information** — Seek out diverse sources, especially those you disagree with
3. **Evaluate evidence** — Is the evidence reliable? What's the source?
4. **Consider alternatives** — What are other possible explanations?
5. **Draw conclusions** — Based on the best available evidence
6. **Test your conclusion** — Does it hold up under scrutiny?

## Applying Critical Thinking Daily

Start small. Pick one area of your life—news consumption, social media, conversations—and apply the framework above.

> "The unexamined life is not worth living." — Socrates

The goal isn't to be right all the time. It's to be less wrong over time.

```python
def think_critically(question):
    evidence = gather_evidence(question)
    alternatives = consider_alternatives(evidence)
    conclusion = evaluate(evidence, alternatives)
    return conclusion
```
